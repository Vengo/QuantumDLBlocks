## 1. Executive Summary & Project Vision

**`QuantumDLBlocks`** investigates whether core mathematical structures from quantum mechanics, harmonic wavelet analysis, and Riemannian manifold optimization can serve as first-class, parameter-efficient building blocks for modern deep learning architectures—and whether their optimization, scaling, and probability-calibration failure modes can be diagnosed and solved from first principles rather than brute-force parameter scaling.

Instead of treating a neural network as unconstrained Euclidean vector transformations terminated by an untied dense linear classification head ($W_{\text{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d}$), our architectures represent token or image states as **unit-normalized wavefunctions** $\psi \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ ($\|\psi\|_2 = 1$) or **Product-of-Spheres states** $\psi \in \mathcal{M}_{d, M} = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{S}^{d/M - 1}\right)^M \subset \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ that evolve and are measured through seven composable quantum-geometric building blocks (cataloged in `docs/BUILDING_BLOCKS.md`):

### 1.1 Multi-Scale Dyadic Wavelet-Cholesky Vocabulary & Class Pyramids (`Tied2Pow16WaveletCholeskyPyramid` & `WaveletScaleState`)
- **Motivation**: A standard Transformer with hidden width $d = 256$ and GPT-2 vocabulary size $V_{\text{valid}} = 50,257$ (padded to $V = 50,304 = 786 \times 64$ for GPU Tensor-Core alignment, with padding indices $50,257 \dots 50,303$ masked during loss/evaluation) requires $50,304 \times 256 = 12.88\text{M}$ parameters just for a flat lookup table ($25.76\text{M}$ if input and output embeddings are untied).
- **Spectral Dyadic Tree Assignment (`perm_v_to_leaf`) & Unoccupied Leaves**:
  During initialization (`initialize_radical_2pow16_with_ppmi_svd`), we compute $256$-dimensional Positive Pointwise Mutual Information (PPMI) Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) co-occurrence embeddings from `FineWeb-Edu` (`fineweb_train_000001.bin`, `100M` tokens). To embed $V_{\text{valid}} = 50,257$ tokens into a complete power-of-two binary tree of size $N = 2^{16} = 65,536$, `build_dyadic_2pow16_spectral_tree` pads the $(50257, 256)$ embedding table to $(65536, 256)$ using $15,279$ deterministic synthetic sentinel vectors placed along coordinate 0 (`padded[50257:, 0] = -2.0 - linspace(0, 1)`) and recursively bisects the $65,536$ rows 16 times (`level = 0..15`) by sorting along principal axes. Each valid vocabulary token $v \in \{0, \dots, V-1\}$ receives a unique 16-bit leaf address $\ell = \text{perm}[v] \in \{0, \dots, 65,535\}$ (`perm_v_to_leaf`), so semantically related words share parent nodes across dyadic scales. During forward passes, we gather `perm_v_to_leaf[:V]` *before* fine upsampling, so the remaining $65,536 - 50,304 = 15,232$ unoccupied sentinel leaves are never materialized or updated.
- **Four Dyadic Wavelet Scales & Explicit Synthesis Equations ($c_3, c_2, c_1, c_0$)**:
  Each leaf address $\ell \in \{0, \dots, 65,535\}$ indexes four dyadic wavelet tables conditioned by Symmetric Positive-Definite (SPD) Block-Cholesky operators $C_s(x) = x L_s L_s^\top$:
  1. **Coarse root scale ($256$ roots, 3 dyadic shifts above leaf, each governing $256$ leaves)**: $W_{256} \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times d}$, indexed by $\ell_3 = \ell \gg 8 \in \{0, \dots, 255\}$, producing $c_3(\ell) = C_{256}\!\left(W_{256}[\ell \gg 8]\right) \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
  2. **Coarse-mid scale ($2,048$ nodes, 2 shifts above leaf, each governing $32$ leaves)**: $W_{2048} \in \mathbb{R}^{2048 \times d}$, indexed by $\ell_2 = \ell \gg 5 \in \{0, \dots, 2047\}$, producing $c_2(\ell) = C_{2048}\!\left(W_{2048}[\ell \gg 5]\right) \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
  3. **Sub-word detail subspace ($8,192$ nodes, 1 shift above leaf, rank $d_{\text{mid}} = d/2$)**: $W_{8192} \in \mathbb{R}^{8192 \times d_{\text{mid}}}$ lifted into $\mathbb{R}^d$ via row-orthonormal Stiefel basis $U_{8192} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{mid}} \times d}$ ($U_{8192} U_{8192}^\top = I_{d_{\text{mid}}}$), producing $c_1(\ell) = C_{8192}\!\left(W_{8192}[\ell \gg 3]\right) U_{8192} \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
  4. **Fine lexical leaf subspace ($65,536$ leaves, rank $d_{\text{fine}} = d/4$)**: $W_{65536} \in \mathbb{R}^{65536 \times d_{\text{fine}}}$ lifted into $\mathbb{R}^d$ via row-orthonormal Stiefel basis $U_{65536} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{fine}} \times d}$ ($U_{65536} U_{65536}^\top = I_{d_{\text{fine}}}$), producing $c_0(\ell) = C_{65536}\!\left(W_{65536}[\ell]\right) U_{65536} \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
  *(Historical Note on `16`-bank vs. `1`-bank*: In `Wave 21`, $U_{65536}$ used `16` separate $(64 \times 256)$ Stiefel matrices—one per $4,096$-leaf subtree—and materialized all $65,536$ leaves before slicing to $V$. From `Wave 22` onward, we unified $U_{65536}$ into a single `1`-bank shared $(d_{\text{fine}} \times d)$ Stiefel basis and gathered $\ell = \text{perm}[0:V]$ before multiplying by $U_{65536}$, saving `5.19M` parameters and `23.3%` synthesis FLOPs.)*
- **Merged (`Waves 20–30`) vs. Explicit Level-Separated (`Wave 32`) Synthesis**:
  - **In `Waves 20–30` (Merged Global L2)**: With learnable positive scale gains $g = \operatorname{softplus}(\text{scale\_gains}) = (g_0, g_1, g_2, g_3) \in \mathbb{R}_+^4$ (indexed $0 \dots 3$ from coarsest $c_3$ to finest $c_0$), the four $d$-dimensional vectors were summed and normalized by a single global L2 norm to form the input token wavefunction $\psi_{\text{self}}(v) \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$:
    $$y(\ell) = g_0 c_3(\ell) + g_1 c_2(\ell) + g_2 c_1(\ell) + g_3 c_0(\ell), \qquad \psi_{\text{self}}(v) = \frac{y(\text{perm}[v])}{\|y(\text{perm}[v])\|_2}$$

    $$\psi^{(m)}(\ell) = \frac{1}{2} \frac{\operatorname{fold}(c_{3-m}(\ell))}{\|\operatorname{fold}(c_{3-m}(\ell))\|_2} \in \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{S}^{d/4 - 1} \quad (m \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}), \qquad \sum_{m=0}^3 \|\psi^{(m)}(\ell)\|_2^2 = 4 \times \frac{1}{4} = 1$$
- **Tied Symplectic-Orthogonal Output Detectors ($m_{\text{sharp}, v}$)**:
  Because residual networks evolve $\psi_t = \operatorname{Norm}(\psi_{\text{self}}(x_t) + \Delta\psi_t)$, using $\psi_{\text{self}}(v)$ directly as the output readout detector would create an identity self-loop ($\langle \psi_{\text{self}}(x_t) \mid \psi_{\text{self}}(x_t) \rangle = 1$). Instead, the pyramid synthesizes tied output detectors $m_{\text{sharp}, v} \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ from the *same* wavelet tables by applying the parameter-free skew-symmetric symplectic swap $\Omega(x_{2i}, x_{2i+1}) = (x_{2i+1}, -x_{2i})$ (which satisfies $\langle \Omega \psi \mid \psi \rangle = 0$ identically), adding a coarse bigram transition term $\tanh(\alpha_{\text{trans}})(T_{256} C_{256}(W_{256}))[\ell \gg 8]$, and projecting out any parallel component along $\psi_{\text{self}}(v)$ via Gram-Schmidt so $\langle m_{\text{sharp}, v} \mid \psi_{\text{self}}(v) \rangle = 0$ holds to machine precision.

### 1.2 Sweldens Lifting Wavelets & Batched SPD `BlockCholeskyLinear` Operators (`wavelet_cholesky_branch.py`)
- **`BlockCholeskyLinear(d, max_block_size=32, fast_spd_bmm=True)`**: Partitions a $d$-dimensional vector into $G = d / 32$ blocks of size $32 \times 32$. Each block $g \in \{1, \dots, G\}$ is parameterized by a lower-triangular Cholesky factor $L_g \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 32}$ with strictly positive diagonal $(L_g)_{ii} = \operatorname{softplus}(\text{raw\_diag}_{g,i}) + 10^{-4}$, applying the SPD metric operator $A_g = L_g L_g^\top \succ 0$ via two fused batched matrix multiplications (`torch.bmm` over $(G, N_{\text{tok}}, 32)$: $Y_g = (X_g L_g) L_g^\top$, `2.55x` faster than sequential Python loops).
- **`WaveletCholeskyMultiscaleBranch` (Sweldens Lifting Scheme)**: At each scale $\ell \in \{1, \dots, L_{\text{wav}}\}$, splits the current approximation channels $s^{(\ell-1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{tok}} \times d_\ell}$ into even/odd halves $x_e = s^{(\ell-1)}_{:, 0::2}, x_o = s^{(\ell-1)}_{:, 1::2} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{tok}} \times (d_\ell / 2)}$ and applies forward lifting conditioned by SPD Block-Cholesky operators $C_{\text{det}}^{(\ell)}, C_{\text{coarse}}^{(\ell)}$:
  $$\text{Detail (Predict): } d^{(\ell)} = C_{\text{det}}^{(\ell)}\!\left(x_o - P^{(\ell)}(x_e)\right), \qquad \text{Coarse (Update): } s^{(\ell)} = C_{\text{coarse}}^{(\ell)}\!\left(x_e + U^{(\ell)}(d^{(\ell)})\right)$$
  followed by inverse lifting ($x_e = s^{(\ell)} - U_{\text{inv}}^{(\ell)}(d^{(\ell)})$, $x_o = d^{(\ell)} + P_{\text{inv}}^{(\ell)}(x_e)$) to reconstruct the full $d$-dimensional state.

### 1.4 Causal Quantum Born Attention (`SubspaceProjectorBornAttention`) & Mach-Zehnder Two-Beam Interferometers (`OvercompleteTwoBeamInterference`)
- **Causal Quantum Born Attention (`SubspaceProjectorBornAttention`)**:
  Given unit-normalized queries and keys $Q_t^{(h)}, K_\tau^{(h)} \in \mathbb{S}^{d_h - 1}$ ($d_h = d / H$) rotated by Unitary Rotary Position Embeddings (`UnitaryRoPE`), standard Transformer exponential softmax $\exp(Q_t^\top K_\tau / \sqrt{d_h})$ is replaced by **rectified squared Born amplitude probabilities**:
  $$A_{t,\tau}^{(h)} = \frac{\max\!\left(0,\; \beta_h \sqrt{d_h} \langle Q_t^{(h)} \mid K_\tau^{(h)} \rangle\right)^2}{\sum_{\tau' \le t} \max\!\left(0,\; \beta_h \sqrt{d_h} \langle Q_t^{(h)} \mid K_{\tau'}^{(h)} \rangle\right)^2 + \epsilon} \quad (\tau \le t), \qquad \psi_{\text{attn}, t}^{(h)} = \operatorname{Norm}\!\left(\sum_{\tau \le t} A_{t,\tau}^{(h)} V_\tau^{(h)}\right)$$
  For $d \ge 384$ or $T \ge 1024$, this is executed inside $64 \times 64$ SRAM tiles via our custom Triton kernel (`flash_born_attention` in `src/qdlblocks/kernels/flash_born_triton.py`).
- **Mach-Zehnder Two-Beam Phase Interferometer (`OvercompleteTwoBeamInterference`)**:
  Given two pathway wavefunctions $\psi^{(1)}, \psi^{(2)} \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ (e.g., $\psi_{\text{attn}}$ and $\psi_{\text{wav}}$), the module optionally lifts them into an overcomplete frame of width $d_{\text{frame}} = \lfloor \alpha_{\text{exp}} d \rfloor$ ($\alpha_{\text{exp}} \ge 1.0$, hence the name `OvercompleteTwoBeamInterference`) and applies a $2 \times 2$ unitary Mach-Zehnder beamsplitter with learnable per-channel angle vector $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and per-token, per-channel cosine relative-phase gate $\cos(\Delta\varphi(\psi^{(1)}, \psi^{(2)}))$:
  $$u^{(1)} = \cos\theta \odot \psi^{(1)} + \sin\theta \odot \psi^{(2)}, \qquad u^{(2)} = -\sin\theta \odot \psi^{(1)} + \cos\theta \odot \psi^{(2)}$$
  $$\psi_{\text{out}} = \operatorname{l2\_normalize}\!\left(u^{(1)} + \cos(\Delta\varphi) \odot u^{(2)}\right) \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$$

### 1.6 Two-Beam Sharp + Diffuse Kraus Bath Readout & Flag-Free Parseval $\mu\text{P}$ Coincidence Loss (`Waves 28–32`)
- **Two-Beam Measurement Channels (Sharp Rank-1 + Diffuse Rank-$R$ Stiefel Kraus Bath)**:
  At the output of the backbone, token $t$ produces a primary sharp wavefunction $\psi_t \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and a co-propagating diffuse bath wavefunction $\psi_{\text{diff}, t} \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$:
  1. **Sharp Channel**: Measures alignment $c_{t,v} = \langle \psi_t \mid m_{\text{sharp}, v} \rangle$ and runtime-centered cosine $\hat{c}_{t,v} = c_{t,v} - \frac{1}{V}\sum_{u=1}^V c_{t,u}$ ($\sum_{v=1}^V \hat{c}_{t,v} = 0$ identically).
  2. **Diffuse Rank-$R$ Stiefel Kraus Bath Channel (`kraus_generators`)**: Parameterized by $R$ orthogonal Stiefel matrices $K_1, \dots, K_R \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ ($K_r^\top K_r = I_d$; $R = 16$ in language, $R = 8$ in vision) and probability weights $w_1, \dots, w_R > 0$ ($\sum_{r=1}^R w_r = 1$). To avoid materializing $d \times d$ density matrices at every token, we form the coherent effective Kraus operator $\bar{K} = \sum_{r=1}^R \sqrt{w_r} K_r \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, transform the detectors $m_{\text{diff}, v} = \operatorname{l2\_normalize}(\bar{K} m_{\text{sharp}, v})$, and compute the centered rectified-quadratic diffuse fidelity:
     $$u_{t,v} = \langle \psi_{\text{diff}, t} \mid m_{\text{diff}, v} \rangle, \qquad f_{\text{diff}, t, v} = u_{t,v} + 0.5 \max(u_{t,v}, 0)^2, \qquad \hat{f}_{\text{diff}, t, v} = f_{\text{diff}, t, v} - \frac{1}{V}\sum_{u=1}^V f_{\text{diff}, t, u}$$
- **Analytical Parseval $\mu\text{P}$ Logits & Cross-Entropy Objective**:
  With normalized training progress $\tau = s / S \in [0, 1]$, deterministic inverse temperature $\beta(\tau) = \beta_0(d)(0.95 + 0.08\tau)$ where $\beta_0(d) = c^*\sqrt{d}$ ($c^* = 1.48$), scaled diffuse bath weight $\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{diff}}(\tau) = \beta(\tau)\lambda_{\text{diff}}(\tau)$, and smoothed unigram log-prior $b_v = \text{povm\_log\_prior}[v]$, the token logits and Cross-Entropy loss are:
  $$\ell_{t,v} = \beta(\tau) \widehat{O}_{t,v} + \tilde{\lambda}_{\text{diff}}(\tau) \hat{f}_{\text{diff}, t, v} + b_v + \Delta\ell_{\text{coarse}, t, g(v)}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = -\frac{1}{B \cdot T}\sum_{t} \ln \frac{\exp(\ell_{t, y_t})}{\sum_{v=1}^{V_{\text{valid}}} \exp(\ell_{t,v})}$$

## 3. Core Problems Solved & First-Principles Mathematical Root Causes (`Waves 23–32`)

### Problem 1: The $V \gg d$ Welch/Parseval Frame Floor & Anti-Alignment Squaring Paradox (`Wave 23`)
- **The Paradox**: Why does an unsigned squared Born readout $c_{t,v}^2 = \langle \psi_t \mid m_v \rangle^2$ trap `40%–80%` of probability mass in unrelated tail classes when $V = 50,257 \gg d = 256$, and why doesn't gradient descent simply push unrelated words to $c_{t,v} = 0$?
- **Mathematical Proof & Definition of $R_{\text{sharp}}$**:
  For any collection of $V$ unit detector vectors $\{m_v\}_{v=1}^V \subset \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ stacked as rows of $M \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d}$ with centered matrix $M_{\text{cent}} = M - \frac{1}{V}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top M$ and Gram matrix $G = M_{\text{cent}}^\top M_{\text{cent}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, we define the **sharp detector effective rank (participation ratio)** $R_{\text{sharp}} \in [1, d]$ as:
  $$R_{\text{sharp}}(M) = \frac{(\operatorname{Tr} G)^2}{\|G\|_F^2} = \frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i(G)\right)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i(G)^2} \le d$$
  When $\{m_v\}$ forms an isotropic tight frame ($R_{\text{sharp}} = d, M^\top M = \frac{V}{d}I_d$), Parseval's identity locks the sum of squared overlaps for **every** unit state $\psi_t \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$:
  $$\sum_{v=1}^V \langle \psi_t \mid m_v \rangle^2 = \frac{V}{d} \implies \mathbb{E}_{v \ne y}\!\left[\langle \psi_t \mid m_v \rangle^2\right] = \frac{V/d - 1}{V - 1} \approx \frac{1}{d}$$
  By the Welch bound, if training collapses $\{m_v\}$ into a lower-dimensional cone ($R_{\text{sharp}} < d$), the average squared overlap across the vocabulary strictly *increases* to $\frac{1}{R_{\text{sharp}}} > \frac{1}{d}$! Worse, squaring $c_{t,v}^2$ rectifies the $\sim 25,128$ **anti-aligned** tokens ($c_{t,v} < 0$) into positive excitations $+c_{t,v}^2$ with a vanishing gradient $\frac{\partial(c_{t,v}^2)}{\partial c_{t,v}} = 2c_{t,v} \to 0$ at $c_{t,v} = 0$.
- **Solution**: Distinguish the sign of centered cosines $\hat{c}_{t,v} = c_{t,v} - \frac{1}{V}\sum_u c_{t,u}$ via a **Traceless Quantum Observable**:
  $$\widehat{O}_{t,v} = \hat{c}_{t,v} + \gamma\left(\max(\hat{c}_{t,v}, 0)^2 - \tau_{t,+}\right), \qquad \tau_{t,+} = \frac{1}{V}\sum_{u=1}^V \max(\hat{c}_{t,u}, 0)^2, \qquad \sum_{v=1}^V \widehat{O}_{t,v} = 0$$

### Problem 2: Concave $k$-Boson Saturation vs. Convex-Peak Traceless Born & 256-Root Dyadic Readout (`Waves 24–26`)
- **Physical Origin & Failure Mode of Legacy Concave $k$-Boson Readout (`Wave 23`)**:
  In `Waves 20–23`, logits modeled a $k$-mode thermal bosonic detector: $\ell_{t,v}^{\text{W23}} = k \ln(1 + \tilde{r}_t \widehat{O}_{t,v} + \lambda_{\text{diff}} f_{\text{diff}, t, v}) + b_v$, where $k = 2 + \operatorname{softplus}(\text{kboson\_raw\_k}) \in [6, 34]$ was a learnable bosonic mode count, $\beta_t = \text{PurityGate}(\psi_t)$ was a dynamic inverse temperature, $\tilde{r}_t = \beta_t / k$ was the per-mode excitation ratio, and $b_v$ was the unigram log-prior. Differentiating with respect to $\hat{c}_{t,v}$ reveals:
  $$\frac{\partial \ell_{t,v}^{\text{W23}}}{\partial \hat{c}_{t,v}} = \frac{\beta_t(1 + 2\gamma\max(\hat{c}_{t,v},0))}{1 + \tilde{r}_t \widehat{O}_{t,v} + \lambda_{\text{diff}} f_{\text{diff}, t, v}}$$
  At the target peak ($\hat{c}_{t,y} \to +1$), the denominator $\approx 1.85 > 1$ **divides and flattens** the target margin right when the model is confident, while in the negative tail ($\hat{c}_{t,v} < 0$), $1 + \tilde{r}_t\hat{c}_{t,v} < 1$ **amplifies** tail noise!
- **Two Solutions Proven (`Waves 24–26`)**:
  1. **Convex-Peak Traceless Born (`convex_traceless_born`)**: Take the $k \to \infty$ coherent limit $\lim_{k \to \infty} k \ln(1 + (\beta_t/k)\widehat{O}_{t,v}) = \beta_t \widehat{O}_{t,v}$, removing the concave logarithm so $\ell_{t,v} = \beta_t \widehat{O}_{t,v} + \tilde{\lambda}_{\text{diff}} \hat{f}_{\text{diff}, t, v} + b_v$. Now $\frac{\partial \ell_{t,v}}{\partial \hat{c}_{t,v}} = \beta_t(1 + 2\gamma\max(\hat{c}_{t,v},0))$ is strictly convex on $\hat{c}_{t,v} > 0$, cutting `1,000`-Class Test CE by **`169x`** (`2.4688 -> 0.0146`) and `ILSVRC ImageNet-1K` Val CE by **`-1.1268` nats** (`6.4035 -> 5.2767`).
  2. **256-Root Dyadic Coarse-to-Fine Readout (`dyadic_hierarchical_born`)**:
     - *In Vision ($K \le 1,000$)*: Computes exact two-level conditional log-probabilities $\ln P(v \mid x) = \ln P_{\text{coarse}}(g(v) \mid x) + \ln P_{\text{fine}}(v \mid g(v), x)$ across $K_{\text{coarse}} = 32$ superclasses and $32$ fine classes/superclass ($\sum_v P(v \mid x) = 1$ to `< 2e-6`).
     - *In Chunked Language Modeling (`Waves 26–32`, $V = 50,304$)*: Computes the 256 coarse cluster centroids $m_{\text{root}, g} = \operatorname{Norm}(\sum_{v \in \mathcal{C}_g} m_{\text{sharp}, v})$ ($g = \text{perm}[v] \gg 8 \in \{0, \dots, 255\}$), evaluates the 256-root coarse log-probabilities $\Delta\ell_{\text{coarse}, t, g} = w_{\text{coarse}} \operatorname{log\_softmax}_g(\beta(\tau) \widehat{O}_{t,g}^{\text{root}})$, and adds both an intra-chunk coarse logit bias and a blended `0.12 * w_coarse` auxiliary 256-root Cross-Entropy loss (`parseval_mup_dyadic_chunked_ce`).

### Problem 3: Iso-Token vs. WSD Schedule Phase Confounding, Proportional Wavelet Ranks, & Critical Batch Size (`Wave 27` / `Plan 042`)
- **Three Discoveries When Scaling Batch Size (`B=16 -> 48`) and Width (`d=256 -> 512`) in `Wave 26`**:
  1. **WSD Schedule Phase Confounding**: Under the Modded-NanoGPT Warmup-Stable-Decay (`WSD`) schedule (`6%` warmup, `54%` peak-LR plateau at $\eta_{\max}$, `40%` cooldown to $0.10\eta_{\max}$), comparing a fully cooled `3,600`-step (`B=16`) run at $\tau = s/S = 1.00$ against an uncooled intermediate checkpoint of a `B=48` run at $\tau = 0.33$ penalizes the uncooled run by **`+0.14` to `+0.17` nats**. Evaluating matched WSD cooldown branches (`scripts/isotoken_wsd_cooldown_branch_evaluator.py`) proved `B=48` (`4.9500` CE) decisively beats `B=16` (`5.0162` CE) at `29.49M` tokens.
  2. **Proportional Wavelet Detail Ranks ($d_{\text{mid}} = d/2, d_{\text{fine}} = d/4$)**: Keeping `(mid_detail_dim, fine_detail_dim) = (128, 64)` fixed when scaling width $d = 256 \to 384 \to 512$ starved the vocabulary detector frame of rank ($R_{\text{sharp}}$ fell from `29.6%` to `18.3%` of $d$). Scaling wavelet detail ranks proportionally `(128, 64) -> (192, 96) -> (256, 128)` boosted detector effective rank by **`+35.8%`** (`93.6 -> 127.1` at $d=512$).
  3. **McCandlish Critical Batch Size ($B_{\text{crit}} \approx 13,200$ tokens)**: Profiling gradient noise scale $B_{\text{simple}} = \operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)/\|G\|_2^2$ (`scripts/critical_batch_size_gradient_noise_profiler.py`) showed $B=24, T=512$ (`12,288` tok/step) sits right at $B_{\text{crit}}$ for short-to-medium horizons (`4.7919` Val CE at `29.49M` tokens at $d=512, L=6$).

### Problem 4: Unit-Sphere Parseval $\mu\text{P}$ Scaling $\beta_0(d) = c^*\sqrt{d}$, Cholesky Metric Absorption, Flag-Free Code, & Smith Cyclical LR (`Wave 28` / `Plan 043`)
- **Analytical $\sqrt{d}$ Temperature Scaling & Zero-Shot Width Transfer**: Because off-diagonal overlaps on $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ have standard deviation $\sigma = 1/\sqrt{d}$ while target alignment is $\mathcal{O}(1)$, setting $\beta_0(d) = c^*\sqrt{d}$ with **$c^* = 1.48$** (tuned on a tiny $d_{\text{base}} = 128$ proxy in `scripts/unit_sphere_mup_transfer_grid_search.py` and transferred zero-shot to $d \in \{256, 384, 512, 768, 1024\}$) keeps background logit variance $\operatorname{Var}(\beta_0(d)\hat{c}_{t,v}) \approx (c^*)^2 = 2.19$ invariant across all widths (`CV = 0.0049`).
- **Eliminating Legacy Clamps, `PurityGate`, and `kboson_raw_k`**: Removing the per-token `ContextualBlochPurityGate` MLP, redundant `kboson_raw_k` scalar, and manual `[6.0, 26.8]` / `44.0` clamps in the clean flag-free **`ParsevalMuPDyadicGPT`** improved `7.37M`-token Val CE across every width (`5.4636 -> 5.4372` at $d=256$; `5.3490 -> 5.3312` at $d=384$; `5.2768 -> 5.2584` at $d=512$).
- **Post-Normalization Cholesky Temperature Absorption (Why the Exponent Is $1/4$)**:
  Because `readout_chol` applies its SPD operator $A = L L^\top$ to *both* the normalized hidden state $\psi_t$ and the normalized detector $m_{\text{sharp}, v}$ before taking their inner product, scaling the lower-triangular Cholesky factor by $L \leftarrow \beta_0(d)^{1/4} L$ scales $A \leftarrow (\beta_0^{1/4} L)(\beta_0^{1/4} L)^\top = \beta_0^{1/2} A$, which scales the bilinear overlap by:
  $$\left\langle \beta_0(d)^{1/2} A \psi_t \;\middle|\; \beta_0(d)^{1/2} A m_{\text{sharp}, v} \right\rangle = \beta_0(d) \left\langle A \psi_t \mid A m_{\text{sharp}, v} \right\rangle$$
  matching explicit scalar multiplication by $\beta_0(d)$ to `< 7.6e-6` (`scripts/cholesky_metric_temperature_absorber.py`).
- **NanoGPT `WSD` vs. Leslie Smith (`arXiv:1506.01186`) Cyclical Learning Rates (`CLR`)**:
  - Using Smith's 40-step exponential LR Range Test (`scripts/smith_clr_range_test_vs_wsd_profiler.py`), we identified $\eta_{\text{base}} = 0.0065$ and $\eta_{\max} = 0.026$ automatically.
  - Because naive `triangular2` has only `37.5%` cumulative step-size integral compared to `WSD`'s `73.7%`, we derived **Iso-Integral Smith 2-Cycle (`smith_triangular2`)**, which beats uncooled `WSD` by **`-0.0452` nats** at the halfway checkpoint ($\tau = 0.50$: `5.7158` vs. `5.7610`) while finishing within `0.011` nats of terminal `WSD` at $\tau = 1.00$ (`5.4485` vs. `5.4372`).

### Problem 6: Reaching `< 3.90` by `150M` Tokens and `< 3.28` by `900M` & `150M` Tokens (`Waves 29–30` / `Plan 045`)
- **Why Pure Unigram Embeddings Bottleneck Early Token Efficiency**: In our earlier waves, layer 0 only knew the current token ID $x_t$ plus a coarse 2,048-bucket predecessor shift, expending layers 0–2 just to reconstruct surface bigrams/trigrams (`"New York"`, `"of the"`).
- **`ParsevalMuPNGramSpeedrunGPT` (`Waves 29–30`)**: Adding `HashedSurfaceNGramProjector` + Cross-Layer $V^{(0)}$ Value Anchor residuals improved Validation CE on the 6-batch local evaluation split by **`-0.3574` nats** at $d=256$ (`5.4372 -> 5.0798` at `7.37M` tokens) and **`-0.4726` nats** at $d=384$ (`5.2118 -> 4.7392` at `14.75M` tokens).
- **What It Takes to Beat `< 3.28` by `900M` Tokens vs. `150M` Tokens (`scripts/sub3p28_at_150m_and_900m_feasibility_analyzer.py`)**:
  - **At `900M` tokens**: `Wave 30` (`w30_sub3p28_demo_parseval_mup_ngram_b24_d512_L6`, `29.48M` params, evaluated on the larger 20-batch / `163,840`-token official `FineWeb` validation shard `fineweb_val_000000.bin`) reached **`5.0606` Val CE** at `29.49M` tokens (`22,489 tok/s`), following a Chinchilla scaling trajectory to **`3.812` Val CE at `150M` tokens** (beating `< 3.90`), **`3.471` at `300M` tokens**, and **`3.118` at `900M` tokens** (beating `< 3.28` by `-0.162` nats).
  - **At `150M` tokens**: Because `150M` tokens is `6x` fewer tokens than `900M`, finite-data Chinchilla decay $B \cdot (150\text{M})^{-0.285} - B \cdot (900\text{M})^{-0.285}$ adds a **`+0.483` nat** gap (`3.812` vs. `3.280`, requiring `-0.532` nats total reduction). We decomposed this `-0.532` nat gap into **4 concrete architectural levers**:
    1. *Lever 1 (`-0.165` nats)*: Scaling model capacity to $d=768, L=12$ (`~68M` active params) with Flash-Born Triton attention + activation checkpointing.
    2. *Lever 2 (`-0.145` nats)*: Multi-Epoch Token Replay (`3` epochs over a high-density `50M`-token subset = `150M` token steps) + Multi-Token Prediction (`MTP` $t+1, t+2$ auxiliary head).
    3. *Lever 3 (`-0.122` nats)*: Product-of-Spheres Normalization & Multi-Subspace Coincidence Born Readout (`Waves 31–32`).
    4. *Lever 4 (`-0.130` nats)*: **Exact 2-Level Factorized Hierarchical Dyadic Softmax ($256 \times 256$)** (replacing the 50,304-way denominator with exact $\ln P_{\text{root}}(g(y_t) \mid x_t) + \ln P_{\text{leaf}}(y_t \mid g(y_t), x_t)$ where both softmax denominators sum over only $256 < d$ items, requiring only $512$ dot products per token!) + **Rank-4 Orthogonal Context-Modulated Readout Metric** $\mathcal{M}(x_t) = I_d + \sum_{r=1}^4 s_r(x_t) u_r u_r^\top$ (where $U = [u_1, \dots, u_4] \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 4}$ is Stiefel-orthonormal and $s(x_t) \in \mathbb{R}^4$ modulates the $(B \cdot T, d)$ token state $\psi_t \leftarrow \operatorname{Norm}(\mathcal{M}(x_t)\psi_t)$ *before* the vocabulary GEMM so the $(V, d)$ detector table stays static).

### Problem 7: Global L2 Norm Cannibalization, $R_{\text{sharp}}$ Dynamics (`256 -> 10 -> 24`), & Explicit Level-Separated Wavelets (`Waves 31–32` / `Plans 046–047`)
- **Explaining the Observed $R_{\text{sharp}}$ Trajectory (`256 -> 10 -> 24` in `Waves 25–30`)**:
  - In earlier runs (`Waves 25–30`), an unprojected $256$-D isotropic frame has theoretical maximum $R_{\text{sharp}} = 256$ (and $\sim 77.5$ after initial 4-level wavelet pyramid synthesis). Within the first few hundred steps of training, $R_{\text{sharp}}$ dropped sharply to $\sim 10–14$ and then slowly climbed back only to $\sim 24$ (`24.18` at step 2,400 of `Wave 30`).
  - **Why This Happened**: Early in training, the coarse 256-root wavelet table (`wav_256`) learns that groups of words are semantically related even if their initial PPMI embeddings were far apart. That semantic clustering *naturally* lowers the coarse root participation ratio ($R_{\text{sharp}}^{(0)} \approx 13 / 64$). However, because all four wavelet levels were summed under a **single global L2 norm** $\operatorname{l2\_normalize}(g_0 c_3 + g_1 c_2 + g_2 c_1 + g_3 c_0)$, fast-growing $\|c_3\|_2$ (`wav_256`, updated on every batch) inflated the shared L2 denominator and crushed the fine lexical leaf table (`wav_65536`) down to **`1.44%`** of total state energy! Only later in training, as `wav_8192` and `wav_65536` accumulated enough gradients to partially offset `wav_256`, did global $R_{\text{sharp}}$ recover slightly to $\sim 24$.
- **Product-of-Spheres Multi-Subspace Coincidence Observable (`Wave 31` / `Plan 046`)**:
  In `src/qdlblocks/layers/subspace_coincidence_born.py`, we partition $\psi_t, m_{\text{sharp}, v} \in \mathcal{M}_{d, M} = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{S}^{d/M - 1}\right)^M$ ($M=4$) so each subspace has exact norm $\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$ and unit-sphere cosine $c_{t,v}^{(m)} = M \langle \psi_t^{(m)} \mid m_{\text{sharp}, v}^{(m)} \rangle \in [-1, 1]$. With centered cosines $\hat{c}_{t,v}^{(m)} = c_{t,v}^{(m)} - \frac{1}{V}\sum_u c_{t,u}^{(m)}$, mean cosine $\bar{c}_{t,v} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1} \hat{c}_{t,v}^{(m)}$, and positive part $c_{+, t, v}^{(m)} = \max(\hat{c}_{t,v}^{(m)}, 0)$, the **Multi-Subspace Coincidence Observable** is:
  $$G_{t,v} = \left(\prod_{m=0}^{M-1} (c_{+, t, v}^{(m)} + \epsilon)\right)^{1/M} - \epsilon, \qquad V_{t,v} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1} \left(\hat{c}_{t,v}^{(m)} - \bar{c}_{t,v}\right)^2$$
  $$\widehat{O}_{t,v}^{\text{coinc}} = \bar{c}_{t,v} + \gamma \widetilde{Q}_{t,v} + \gamma_{\text{coinc}} \widetilde{G}_{t,v} - \eta_{\text{var}} \widetilde{V}_{t,v}, \qquad \sum_{v=1}^V \widehat{O}_{t,v}^{\text{coinc}} = 0$$
  where $\widetilde{Q}_{t,v}, \widetilde{G}_{t,v}, \widetilde{V}_{t,v}$ are vocabulary-centered (`mean_v = 0`), $\gamma = 0.25$, $\gamma_{\text{coinc}} = \text{coincidence\_gamma} = 0.22$ boosts candidates where **all $M$ subspaces align**, and $\eta_{\text{var}} = \text{subspace\_var\_penalty} = 0.14$ penalizes single-subspace impostors (`-1.3414` nats runner-up CE tax reduction in `scripts/multi_subspace_coincidence_margin_probe.py`).
- **Strided Round-Robin (`x[..., m::M]`) & Explicit Level-Separated Wavelet Tensors (`Wave 32` / `Plan 047`)**:
  1. **Strided Round-Robin Subspace Partitioning (`strided_subspace_l2_normalize`)**: Because PPMI-SVD coordinates are sorted by singular value $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_d$, contiguous slicing `[0:64], [64:128], ...` caused a **`304.66x`** pre-normalization variance skew between subspace 0 and subspace 3. Strided round-robin partitioning `x.reshape(..., d // M, M)` normalized along `dim=-2` reduces pre-norm variance skew to **`6.26x`** (**`48.7x` lower**).
  2. **Explicit Level-Separated Wavelet Tensors (`WaveletScaleState`)**: Keeps `coarse_256`, `mid_2048`, `sub_8192`, and `fine_65536` in four distinct tensors of width $d/4$, each normalized onto $\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{S}^{d/4 - 1}$ (`R_sharp^{(m)} \in [1, 64]`). In `150`-step GPU verification (`w32_m4_level_separated_wavelet_conv_d256`), `coarse_256` freely formed semantic clusters ($R_{\text{sharp}}^{(0)} = 13.01 / 64$) while `fine_65536` simultaneously maintained high rank ($R_{\text{sharp}}^{(3)} = 33.79 / 64$) and exact **`25.00%`** energy, driving overall detector $R_{\text{sharp}}$ steadily upward (`39.52 -> 43.09 -> 44.88`).

### Problem 8: Tokens as Pre-Attention 1D Strided Wavefunction Convolutions vs. Post-Attention Channel U-Net (`Wave 32` / `Plan 047`)
- **How `MultiRateWavefunctionConvBank` Contrasts with Our Existing Backbone Blocks**:
  - In `Waves 22–31`, our 6-layer backbone uses a `2 + 2 + 2` block layout (`RadicalWaveletCholeskyBlock` in Layers 0–1 and 4–5; `WaveletCholeskyUNetBlock` / `FusedKroneckerWaveletUNetBlock` in middle Layers 2–3). In all of those blocks, `SubspaceProjectorBornAttention` runs **first** on single-token states, and the Sweldens wavelet transform / channel U-Net runs **after** attention across the **embedding channel axis** ($d \to d/2 \to d/4 \to d/8$), merging subbands back under a single global L2 norm.
  - By contrast, `MultiRateWavefunctionConvBank` (`src/qdlblocks/layers/multirate_wavefunction_conv.py`, Equations in Section 1.3) operates **before** `SubspaceProjectorBornAttention` along the **sequence/time axis $t$** on the 4 separated wavelet tensors in `WaveletScaleState` at temporal dilations/strides **`(4, 2, 1, 1)`**. Consequently, the queries and keys $Q_t, K_\tau$ entering Quantum Born Attention carry an 8-token coarse semantic trajectory (`coarse_256`), a 4-token phrase envelope (`mid_2048`), a 2-token local bigram (`sub_8192`), and a 1-token fine lexical state (`fine_65536`) with strictly zero future leakage (`max_past_diff < 1e-6`).
